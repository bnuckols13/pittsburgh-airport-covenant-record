Where the numbers come from, and what this cannot tell you

Every baseline is the Authority’s own figure from the April 2025 Official Statement at os-2025ab PDF 202 (printed B-16), carried in 02-data/coverage-table.csv. Designations come from Exhibit E at PDF 316 by way of 02-data/other-pledged-revenue.csv, the years the Authority committed to from PDF 67, and the flow of funds and the 25 percent cap from PDF 31, 32 and 34. The operating savings the forecast credits to the Terminal Modernization Program are from Exhibit D, in 02-data/tmp-savings.csv.

The one figure not printed as such is the first block, what the airport earns. It is Net Revenues less the designated money, a subtraction of two published rows. The indenture makes the distinction itself: Other Pledged Revenues are “moneys, not constituting Revenues, that are designated.”

The stress scenario is the statement’s own second forecast, from the same table, in 02-data/stress-case.csv. Traffic underperforms, coverage falls about a hundredth, and the charge to airlines rises more than four dollars. The stress column prints no enplanement series, so the charge computed here under that scenario runs on base-case boardings and comes out low, and the figure the column itself prints is shown beside it.

One number here is not the Authority’s. The share of a shortfall that reaches the covenant test has no printed split behind it, so it starts at none, where the page reproduces the statement exactly. Raise it and the test begins to move with traffic and with cost, on an assumption the control names as ours.

This is arithmetic on the Authority’s own rows. At the baseline it reproduces all three printed ratio series and the charge to the cent, and the build refuses to write the page when it does not. Moving a control shows what follows from an assumption, and says nothing about how likely that assumption is.

It cannot tell you what the Authority will decide, what Harrisburg will do to the gaming appropriation, or what traffic will be.

This page never says default, breach or violation. The indenture lets the Coverage Account count toward the 1.25 test, and on that test the Authority’s forecasts comply in every year. Missing the covenant once would not be an event of default: the indenture requires the Authority to hire a consultant, take its advice and raise its rates, and only a second consecutive miss after rates have gone up is an Event of Default.
