What they said, and what they filed

The reporting on Pittsburgh International's terminal financing rests on documents the Airport Authority publishes and files. This page lets you walk the record four ways: along the years, through the 1.25 test one layer at a time, down to the page a number sits on, and across every claim the story makes. Nothing here is drawn by hand. Every figure comes from a data file in 02-data/, every filing is hashed, every clip is an archived capture, and the command at the foot of the page rebuilds all of it.

Two

The 1.25 test, taken apart

The bond covenant is a margin, not a payment. Scroll, and the forecast comes apart into the three kinds of money that meet it.

The promise

When the Authority borrowed to build the terminal it promised the people buying its debt that each year's revenue available for the payments would be at least 1.25 times the payments. The consultant's forecast, filed with the 2025 bonds, prints the ratio for six years: 1.60, 1.37, 1.36, 1.37, 1.43 and 1.44. Every year clears the line.

Take away the deposit the Authority elects to make

The indenture lets a Coverage Account count toward the test. It is filled from revenue at the Authority's monthly discretion and capped at a quarter of the year's debt payments. The forecast assumes it at that ceiling in five of the six years. The bond statement says the rates charged before the terminal opened fund its required increase, so the carriers pay into it. Without it, the same rows give 1.35, 1.12, 1.13, 1.12, 1.18 and 1.19. Five of six fall below 1.25. The division is this record's.

Take away the state's gaming money

Footnote 1 on the forecast table says Net Revenues include Other Pledged Revenues, which from 2025 is entirely Gaming Act money the Authority designates into the pledge. Subtract it and what the airport earns from running the airport, after expenses, is 1.20, 0.98, 1.00, 0.99, 1.06 and 1.06. In 2026 and 2028 operations do not cover debt service at all.

Everything ends in 2028

Under the airlines' January 2025 vote, the Authority committed $11.575 million a year of that gaming money for 2026 through 2028. The forecast carries the same figure into 2029 and 2030 on nothing firmer than an expectation. The airline agreement that makes the carriers responsible for the shortfall ends Dec. 31, 2028. The bonds run to 2056.

The covenant is met

The indenture does not ask where the money comes from, and on the test it sets, the forecast complies in every year it covers. The finding is what the ratio is on pledged net revenues alone, in those words, and what is underneath that.
